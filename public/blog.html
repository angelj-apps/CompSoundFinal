Building Garden Groovebox in the Browser

What it is

The Garden Groovebox is a 16-step sequencer that plays short FM notes and can rewrite its own pattern under scale constraints. You perform with three levers at once: timbre (FM ratio and index), time (BPM + step grid), and structure (generate / mutate / locks).

It is deliberately small, but the interaction is not “a wall of sliders.” The primary FM control is a 2D performance pad (ratio × index), patterns can be painted, and a Living score canvas closes an audiovisual loop: hits bloom marks, unlocked marks drift, and each bar can write pitch back into the sequencer.

Three things grew on top of that loop. A Resonance field turns every sounding note into light, colored by pitch and shaped by the carrier waveform. A melody stack lets you save a melody (notes plus the entire instrument around them) and loop it underneath the one you are editing. And a season clock gives the garden weather, so how far the marks wander is no longer a constant.

Signal flow

Each scheduled hit builds a disposable FM voice:

ModulatorOsc ──► GainNode (index) ──► CarrierOsc.frequency
      ▲                                 │
      └──── FeedbackGain ◄──────────────┤
                                        │
                              CarrierOsc ──► Lowpass ──► GainNode (amp env) ──► Master ──► Analyser ──► destination
  • Carrier frequency comes from the step’s MIDI note (440 * 2^((midi - 69) / 12)).
  • Modulator frequency is carrierHz * ratio, the classic simple-FM relationship.
  • Index is the GainNode’s gain in hertz of frequency deviation. Connecting that gain to carrier.frequency is AudioParam modulation.
  • Both amplitude and index are enveloped so each hit blooms then settles.
  • Cutoff, feedback, and separate carrier/mod waveforms push the same graph into a more playable instrument without changing the class technique.

Scheduling

Transport uses a standard look-ahead loop (~25 ms tick, ~120 ms horizon) against AudioContext.currentTime:

  1. While the next step time falls inside the horizon, schedule that step.
  2. Step period is a sixteenth note: (60 / bpm) / 4 seconds.
  3. Swing delays odd-indexed 16ths into each pair for a groove feel.
  4. Each step carries a play probability; the scheduler may skip sounding a written note.
  5. Active-step highlighting is delayed until the step’s audio time so the UI does not jump early during look-ahead.

Because notes are scheduled into the future, moving Index / Ratio / Cutoff mid-pattern affects new voices with the current slider values without tearing down the clock.

The scheduler also carries stack layers: saved melodies set to play take their own step on the same clock, each with the FM voice it was saved with. When several notes land on one step, voice amplitude scales by 1 / sqrt(N) so stacking four layers does not clip the master bus. The working melody can be muted independently, which is how you audition the stack without the melody you are editing.

The master bus feeds an AnalyserNode drawn to a canvas scope so FM brightness is visible as well as audible.

Generation model

Steps are { note: number | null, locked: boolean }.

Generate (unlocked steps only):

  • Build a pitch set from tonic + scale across a couple of octaves.
  • Decide rest vs note with a user-facing density control (downbeats slightly denser).
  • Prefer a light first-order Markov sample when the current pattern has transitions; otherwise weighted random.
  • Status text reports markov vs weighted pick counts so the outside-class technique is visible in the UI.
  • Undo restores the previous pattern after generate/mutate.

Mutate (unlocked steps only, per-step probability):

  • Rest → maybe become a scale tone.
  • Note → become a rest, or nudge ±1 degree in the pitch set.

Locks let you pin a motif and ask the machine to fill around it. That interaction is the compositional point: generation is a collaborator with veto power, not a one-shot randomizer.

The garden and its weather

The living score keeps one mark per step column. Marks bloom when their step sounds, unlocked marks drift, and once per bar the unlocked marks write their height back into the pattern as scale degrees. Draw on the canvas to plant a pitch, click a mark again to clear it, right-click a column to lock it.

Drift used to be governed entirely by the FM voice (ratio set the step size, index set the chaos, swing added wobble), which meant the garden was equally restless forever. Climate adds the missing variable:

  • A season advances every four bars while the transport runs.
  • Each season declares a temperature target plus its own drift, damping, wind, sprout/wither odds, tone tint, and palette.
  • Temperature eases 25% of the remaining gap per bar. It never jumps, so a season boundary lands mid-glide and spring reads as warming up rather than a switch flipping.
  • Temperature, not the season, is what actually drives everything downstream.

That easing has a pleasant consequence worth measuring. Because four bars at 25% closes only about 68% of the gap, temperature never reaches its target; it settles into a stable limit cycle between 0.20 and 0.82. The seasons stay distinct forever without ever hitting the extremes, and the whole year is a single line of arithmetic.

Temperature then fans out into four places:

  1. Drift: equilibrium speed ranges from ~0.08x in winter to ~2.1x at peak summer, relative to the old constant. Damping matters as much as step size here: raising both at once compounds, and the first tuning pass made summer about five times too violent.
  2. Growth: unlocked rests may sprout a pitch one scale step from their nearest neighbour; unlocked notes may wither back to rest. Locked steps are untouched and note count is held between 2 and 13 of 16, so the garden can neither empty out nor fill solid. Growth runs mid-bar so it never shares a step with write-back.
  3. Palette: mark and soil colors blend between the outgoing and incoming season by the same eased factor, so the picture glides with the sound.
  4. Tone: cutoff and amp decay get a multiplier, clamped to each slider's range and never written back to the inputs. Winter is dark and long, summer bright and short. The tint goes neutral when the transport stops, so an idle instrument always auditions exactly as its sliders read.

A Bias slider shifts the entire year warmer or colder without fighting the animation, which a self-moving slider would.

Design choices

Why FM instead of a sample kit?

It makes the class technique audible. Turning Index from 0 to a few hundred Hz is an unmistakable timbre lesson, and it photographs well in a four-minute demo.

Why Markov-lite instead of a full grammar?

A transition table seeded from the current pattern keeps generation related to what you are already hearing. It is easy to explain, easy to abuse (lock → generate), and clearly “not covered in a basic Web Audio lab.”

Why seeds (Pulse / Sparse / Climb)?

Cold-start problem. Presets load pattern + BPM + FM defaults so the first Start is already a piece, not an empty grid.

Why does saving a melody save the whole instrument?

Saving only the notes was a trap: a melody recalled without its FM voice, tempo, and generate settings sounded nothing like the thing you saved. A save is a scene (notes plus pad, tone extras, tempo/level, generate, and climate bias), so recalling it restores the moment, and a looping layer keeps its own voice while it plays under whatever you are editing now.

Why seasons instead of more sliders?

The garden already had four or five plausible new knobs (wind, moisture, gravity toward the tonic, crowding). A season is a bundle of them, so one automatic factor buys variety that five manual sliders would have made into homework.

Try this path

  1. Load Pulse, press Start.
  2. Sweep Index while it runs, and watch the Resonance bursts grow with it.
  3. Draw a line on the living score, then right-click to lock a few steps.
  4. Mutate, then Generate: the status line tells you whether it picked markov or weighted.
  5. Wait out a season change and watch the marks loosen or stiffen.
  6. Save the melody, press ▶ to loop it under a new line, then ❚❚ Home to hear the save alone.
  7. Compare Sparse and Climb.

That path is the whole thesis: DSP you can hear, scheduling you can trust, generation you can steer, and a picture that composes back.
